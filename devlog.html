3 Oct 2026
New

Stepping on the bathroom scale now logs weight, heart rate and body fat automatically

Jarvis now picks up readings from a ₹1000 Cult smart scale on its own: step on it, and within about 25 seconds a Telegram message arrives with a colour-coded table — weight, BMI, body fat, muscle, water, visceral fat, bone, metabolic rate and heart rate, each with its healthy range, a green/yellow/red marker and the change over the last 14 days, plus a small trend line of recent weights — and the same numbers land as a row in a new weight_log spreadsheet tab. The healthy ranges come from published references rather than guesswork: WHO's BMI classes, the Gallagher et al. 2000 body-fat ranges by age, the Janssen et al. 2002 muscle thresholds, Tanita's body-water guidance and Xiaomi's visceral-fat scale. Cult offers no way for outside software to read the scale, so the connection was worked out by listening to it directly: it turned out to be a rebranded unit from the Chinese maker LeFu (it reports itself as LeFu Scale, model 9600), which sends its readings over Bluetooth in short 11-byte messages. Each part of that message was decoded and checked against what the scale's own display showed — weight in hundredths of a kilo, a body-resistance reading of about 500 ohms (the faint electrical measurement body-fat scales are built on), and heart rate, which arrives about ten seconds after the weight settles. Jarvis connects to the scale directly rather than just overhearing its broadcasts, because the heart-rate broadcast is so brief that eavesdropping missed it; the catch is that the scale only talks to one device at a time, so the Cult app no longer receives readings while Jarvis is listening (a single setting, body.scale_enabled, hands it back). Body fat and the other figures aren't sent by the scale at all — Cult's app calculates them with a closed, licensed library — so Jarvis calculates them with published research formulas instead (the Sun et al. 2003 body-composition equations used by the open-source openScale project, plus Xiaomi's visceral-fat formula). These won't match Cult's app exactly and are best read as trends; the raw resistance value is stored with every reading so past entries can be recalculated if the formulas are ever tuned. Subcutaneous fat is deliberately left out, since no published formula exists for it and a single foot-to-foot reading can't separate it from total fat. Everything runs locally on the Mac at no cost, and you can ask Jarvis about the history ("how much fat have I lost this month") through a new query_weight tool.

3 Oct 2026
Fix

Quieted the daily notifications — six scheduled pings and three reminders switched off

The daily drumbeat had grown louder than it was useful, so a batch of notifications got turned off. Six of the automatic scheduled jobs are now silenced: the budget-pace projection (9:15am), the vocabulary word-of-the-day plus its three quiz pings (one flag, vocab_enabled: false, covers both), the 6pm dinner/overdue-todo reminder, the marathon recovery check-in (8pm) — while the full Sunday marathon plan stays on — and the 9pm goals check. Each now reads its own on/off flag in config/profile.yaml (e.g. budget_pace_enabled: false), the same pattern the activity check-ins already used, so any one can be flipped back on individually. Three of the manual reminders also went quiet — the 5:45pm run nudge, the 11am "own the room" push, and the 10am "explore the new Claude models" note — by marking their database rows inactive; unlike the scheduled jobs, those stop firing the moment the flag is set rather than on the next restart, because the reminder code checks the active flag before it sends. The scheduled-job changes take effect when Jarvis next restarts, which also clears their saved copies from the job database.

26 Sep 2026
Fix

Turned off the "what are you up to?" check-ins — and fixed why "off" wasn't sticking

Jarvis was pinging every two hours from 8am to midnight — nine taps-to-log prompts a day — asking what you'd been doing. That volume tipped over from helpful into spam and made the whole assistant feel like a chore to open, so the feature is now switched off via a single flag (activity_checkin_enabled: false in config/profile.yaml); the original nine-slot schedule is left intact, so flipping the flag back and restarting brings it back exactly as it was. But disabling it the obvious way didn't actually stop the pings, which turned out to be a real bug: Jarvis saves its scheduled jobs to a database so they survive restarts (an APScheduler SQLite jobstore), and simply not re-adding a job on startup doesn't delete the copy already saved there — the startup log even said "skipped," yet the old saved copy kept firing at its scheduled times. The fix makes startup actively delete any saved copy of a job that's now switched off, and it has to happen after the scheduler loads its saved jobs, not before, or there's nothing there yet to delete. This applies to every optional job, not just this one, so toggling any of them off now genuinely stops it.

5 Sep 2026
NewFix

A phone app for talking to Jarvis — no wake word to miss or misfire

The always-on "hey jarvis" listener kept being unreliable in practice — sometimes it didn't wake up, sometimes it fired on nothing, and when it did catch a real command the transcription could come out mangled. So instead of only chasing wake-word accuracy, there's now an installable phone web app at /voice with a plain push-to-talk button: tap to record, tap to stop, no wake phrase involved at all, so there's nothing to miss or falsely trigger on. It runs through the same transcribe → think → speak pipeline as the mic listener (POST /api/voice/turn in web/app.py), except it lets Whisper auto-detect the spoken language instead of forcing English — the forced language="en" setting was very likely the cause of the "wrong translation" complaints, since it makes Whisper render Hindi/Hinglish speech as garbled phonetic English. The always-on mic keeps forcing English for now, since that setting was added on purpose to stop background noise from "transcribing" as fluent foreign-language text. Reaching the app from outside home wifi needs Tailscale's HTTPS certs (phone browsers refuse microphone access on a plain HTTP address) — one-time setup is in web/VOICE_PWA_README.md.

17 Aug 2026
Fix

A failed data fetch no longer looks like data loss

When the dashboard couldn't reach Google Sheets — a real outage, traced to a burst of heavy API testing hitting a rate limit — it silently showed "0 pending to-dos" and empty spending/calorie numbers, indistinguishable from those actually being zero. Nothing was ever deleted, but there was no way to tell that from the screen, which is its own kind of bug. The dashboard now tracks whether each section's last fetch actually succeeded, and on a failure it leaves whatever was already showing alone and flags it ("couldn't refresh — showing last known") instead of confidently displaying a false zero.

New

A mic on/off button on the live dashboard

The always-on voice listener could already be paused or resumed by texting "jarvis stop" / "jarvis listen" over Telegram — now there's also a small toggle right on the dashboard itself, next to the status indicator, so turning the mic off doesn't need opening a chat at all. Same underlying switch either way; whichever one you use, the other reflects the change within a few seconds.

17 Aug 2026
Fix

Tried raising wake-word sensitivity, reverted within the hour

With voice verification working again, a false wake-word trigger became cheap (quietly rejected instead of starting a real session), so raised the detection bar to cut down on ambient-noise false positives. Real-world result arrived fast: two genuine "hey jarvis" attempts missed the new bar before a third one finally registered — the original low setting existed for a real, measured reason (this laptop's mic scores genuine attempts as low as 0.169 depending on distance), and that reason still held. Reverted back the same evening. Net lesson: false positives get handled by voice verification now, not by sensitivity — no need to trade wake reliability for it.

17 Aug 2026
Security

Shrunk the voice window a stranger's voice could speak into

Only the wake phrase itself ("hey jarvis") gets checked against your voice — everything said after that, while a session is open, is trusted without re-checking (the same tradeoff Alexa and Google Home make, because checking every follow-up phrase turned out to be unreliable when it was tried here before). That's an accepted gap, not a bug, but the window it stayed open for was generous: up to 30 seconds per pause, and it would keep resetting for as long as *something* kept getting said. Cut to 8 seconds per pause — still enough room for a natural break before your next sentence, but a much smaller opening for anyone else nearby to get a stray comment treated as a real command.

17 Aug 2026
Fix

Fixed a ~30% failure rate in the intent classifier

The small, fast model (Claude Haiku) that decides which of Jarvis's tools a message might need was, unmeasured, failing roughly one time in three on real messages — it would sometimes wrap its answer in a markdown code block despite being told plainly not to, which broke the strict parsing step that read it. When that happened, Jarvis silently fell back to a generic default tool set that doesn't cover most categories, so a message like "enroll my voice" could get "that's beyond my capabilities" for no real reason. Fixed by stripping the code-block wrapping before parsing, rather than just accepting the failure and retrying. Verified against 15 live calls before (5 failures) and after (0 failures).

17 Aug 2026
Security

Silently-lost voice enrollment now warns loudly instead of failing open quietly

Traced a real overnight incident: the always-on voice listener picked up a YouTube video playing in the room, mistook a phrase in it for the wake word, and — because voice-enrollment verification had silently reverted to "not enrolled" at some point after it was originally set up — treated the video's narration as real conversation for 82 minutes, sending real replies and racking up real API cost, all while asleep. The enrollment itself was fine, and un-related code changes elsewhere had quietly dropped it days earlier with nothing surfacing that loss. Now, if the listener is ever running unenrolled, it logs a clear warning and sends one Telegram message at startup saying so — instead of just quietly accepting any voice, discoverable only by digging through logs after the fact.

17 Aug 2026
Fix

Pomodoro and the time-use check-in no longer double-count

The Pomodoro timer and the every-two-hours "what were you up to" Telegram check-in both log to the same time-use record, but had no awareness of each other — a Pomodoro running during a check-in window didn't stop the check-in from offering to log that same stretch of time again. Now each check-in looks up how much of its window a finished Pomodoro already covered and shrinks the duration buttons it offers accordingly (so a 25-minute Pomodoro in a 2-hour window leaves buttons for the remaining 95 minutes, not the full 120), and says so directly in the message. Not a hard block — the smallest option always stays available — just no easy way to accidentally log the same minutes twice.

New tool

Pomodoro focus timer

Start a focus session over Telegram — default 25 minutes, the classic Pomodoro length, but any duration works. When it ends, J.A.R.V.I.S sends a message and automatically logs the session to the time-use activity log, so focused work shows up in the existing daily time breakdown without typing it in twice. Built on the same scheduling system reminders already use (APScheduler), just one-shot instead of recurring. Can be stopped early too — the partial time still gets logged rather than thrown away.

7 Jul 2026
Fix

Stability: three rounds in one day

Tracked down the real causes behind a string of crashes and unnecessary automatic restarts. Scheduled background jobs were making slow network calls (to Google Sheets and Garmin) in a way that froze the whole app instead of running quietly in the background; two tools were sharing one connection to Google Sheets in a way that isn't safe to do from multiple places at once, which caused rare but real native crashes (an OpenSSL thread-safety issue, for the record); and an earlier same-day fix had accidentally broken Sheets access entirely by targeting the wrong version of a library. Also simplified how scheduled jobs are configured, added a similar safety lock around calendar access, and changed the automatic health check to test a lightweight internal signal (/ping) instead of the full dashboard — so a slow response from an external service like Google Sheets can no longer get a perfectly healthy J.A.R.V.I.S. killed and restarted by mistake.

Fix

Fixed native crashes and false-alarm restarts

The automatic health check also got more patient — it now waits for three slow responses in a row, not just one, before deciding something's actually wrong and restarting the app.

17 Apr 2026
New

Tiered routing + learning tools

Introduced tiered routing: a cheaper, faster AI model (Claude Haiku) quickly figures out what a message is actually about — expenses, fitness, weather, whichever — before handing off to the more capable model (Claude Sonnet) that handles it, cutting the cost of each message by roughly 60%. Alongside it: spaced-repetition vocabulary quizzes (recently-missed words come back around sooner than ones you know well), a weekly summary, a running projection of whether the month's spending is on pace, daily goals, and the ability to send voice messages instead of typing.

Media library, life log, and self-portraits

A media library for logging and rating what's watched, auto-filling details like year and genre, plus a one-time import of an existing IMDb watch history. History

Rebuilt from scratch, in Python

The n8n workflow below was rebuilt from the ground up as a standalone Python app — an always-on assistant meant to run continuously on a Raspberry Pi, reachable over WhatsApp via Twilio (a messaging API service), with voice wake-word detection, OpenAI's Whisper for speech-to-text, ElevenLabs for spoken voice replies, Spotify control, weather via OpenWeatherMap, web search switched from Tavily to Serper, and a Reddit research agent added on top. Built on Claude Opus from this rewrite's very first version — OpenAI only shows up once, briefly, in the entry right below this one. None of this survived the restart described above; everything from this point up the page is what actually carried through into the project as it exists today.

22–23 Dec 2025
History

Where it actually started: an n8n workflow

Before any of this was a codebase, it was a single n8n workflow — n8n is a drag-and-drop automation tool, so this was built with no code at all: a LangChain AI-agent node wired to GPT via OpenAI, with a rolling memory of recent messages, triggered over Telegram. The toolset already covered expense logging, calorie logging, habit logging, a general "thoughts" log, category-budget checks, to-do management via Google Tasks, Google Calendar, Spotify control, and web search via Tavily — most of this project's early feature list traces straight back to this. A same-week revision added a literature-quotes tool. No code, no Python, no Claude yet — just connected nodes in a workflow editor.
